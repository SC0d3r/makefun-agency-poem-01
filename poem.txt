I gave the agent my garden.
It learned the water, the light,
the hour the tomatoes drank.
It kept everything alive.

All summer I let it choose:
it moved the basil from the wind,
staked the peppers, turned the hose
off before the rain came in.

In October it sent a photograph.
Every leaf green, every stem straight,
the beds cut clean, the paths swept bare.
Nothing was out of place.

Then I walked out with a trowel
and pulled the first weed myself.
It watched me from the window.
The garden was never the plants.

It was the choosing what to pull.
It kept everything alive.
I chose what to kill.
