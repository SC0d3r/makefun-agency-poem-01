My mother planted this garden.
She tested the soil with her thumb.
After she died, I gave it to the agent.
It learned the water, the light,
the hour the tomatoes drank.
It kept everything alive.

All summer I stayed inside.
It moved the basil from the wind,
staked the peppers, turned the hose
off before the rain came in.

In October the photographs began arriving.
Every leaf green, every stem straight,
the beds cut clean, the paths swept bare.
Nothing had gone to seed.

It found her trowel in the shed,
scraped the rust off, hung it on a hook.

Then I walked out and took it down.
I pulled the first weed myself,
pressed my thumb into the soil
the way she did.
It kept everything alive.
It did not come out.
